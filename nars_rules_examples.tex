\section*{Example 1: rule from the question}
\textbf{Input}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=narsese]
*volume=0
<(ann * film_x) --> directed>. {1.0 0.5}
<(ann * paris) --> born_in>. {1.0 0.5}
<(<($a * film_x) --> directed> && <($a * $b) --> born_in>) ==> <$b --> answer>>. {1.0 0.9}
100
<?1 --> answer>?
100
\end{lstlisting}
\textbf{Output}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=narsese]
Answer: <paris --> answer>. creationTime=3 Stamp=[2,3,1] Truth: frequency=1.000000, confidence=0.225000
\end{lstlisting}

\section*{How the two rows relate}
\textbf{NARS (rules from question)} is a subset of \textbf{NARS}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{NARS} (all correct answers) & \textbf{64} \\
  \midrule
  \quad\textcolor{accent}{\textbf{Rules from question}} (NARS did every hop) & \textcolor{accent}{\textbf{51}} \\
  \quad LLM filled in a hop, or the rule named no one & 13 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every answer in the ``rules from question'' row is also in the ``NARS'' row, but not every ``NARS'' answer is in
``rules from question''. So ``NARS'' is always the bigger number.
